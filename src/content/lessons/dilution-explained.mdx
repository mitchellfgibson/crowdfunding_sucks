**Dilution** is what happens to your ownership percentage when a company issues
new shares. It is not automatically bad — but if you don't understand it, you can
end up owning a tiny sliver of something you thought you owned a real piece of.

## The mechanics

Your percentage = your shares ÷ total shares. When a company raises a new round,
it *creates and sells new shares*. The total goes up, so your percentage goes
down — even though your share count never changed.

> You own 100,000 of 1,000,000 shares = 10%. The company issues 500,000 new
> shares in a round. Now there are 1,500,000 shares and you own 100,000 ÷
> 1,500,000 ≈ **6.7%**. You were diluted ~33% — without selling anything.

## Good dilution vs. bad dilution

Dilution can still leave you better off. What matters is whether the company's
value grew by *more* than your percentage shrank.

- **Healthy:** the round is priced higher than the last one ("up round"). Your
  smaller slice is of a bigger pie.
- **Painful:** a **down round** (priced lower than before) dilutes you *and*
  signals the company is worth less. Crowdfunding investors rarely have the
  protections — like anti-dilution clauses — that professional investors negotiate.

## The hidden dilution: SAFEs, notes, and the option pool

Even before a priced round, dilution is often already baked in:

- **SAFEs and convertible notes** convert into shares later, usually at a
  discount. Several stacked SAFEs can dilute founders and early investors more
  than expected when they all convert at once.
- **Option pool expansions** create new shares to hire employees.

## What to check

- Is the current raise an **up round** or **down round** vs. the last price?
- How many **SAFEs/notes** are outstanding, and at what caps/discounts?
- Does the company expect to raise **several more rounds**? More rounds = more
  dilution ahead.
- Are there any **anti-dilution** terms — and do they apply to *your* class of
  shares (usually they don't, for crowdfunding common stock)?

Dilution is normal. Being *surprised* by it is the avoidable mistake.
