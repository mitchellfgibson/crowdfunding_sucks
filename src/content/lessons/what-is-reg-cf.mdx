Equity crowdfunding exists because of a handful of SEC rules created after the
2012 JOBS Act. They let private companies raise money from the general public —
not just wealthy "accredited" investors. The two you'll see most are **Regulation
Crowdfunding (Reg CF)** and **Regulation A+ (Reg A)**.

## Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF)

- Lets a company raise up to a capped amount per year from the public.
- Open to **anyone**, not just accredited investors.
- How much *you* can invest is limited based on your income and net worth — a
  built-in guardrail.
- Must run through an **SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer** (the
  platforms in our [directory](/portals/)).
- Requires a public disclosure filing — the **Form C** (see the
  [Form C lesson](/lessons/reading-a-form-c/)).

## Regulation A+ (Reg A)

- Allows **larger** raises than Reg CF.
- Also open to the general public.
- Heavier disclosure and SEC qualification — closer to a "mini-IPO."
- Often used by more established companies raising bigger amounts.

## What the rules do — and don't — protect

The rules are about **disclosure and access**, not safety. They make sure a
company tells you certain things and limits how much you can lose relative to
your means. They do **not**:

- vet whether the business is good,
- guarantee the valuation is fair,
- promise any liquidity (you often can't sell for years, if ever).

> The regulation getting you *in* the door says nothing about whether you should
> walk through it. That judgment is on you — which is what the rest of these
> lessons are for.

## Why it matters for evaluating a deal

Knowing which rule a raise uses tells you what disclosures to expect, how big the
raise can be, and which protections exist. A Reg CF deal will have a Form C to
read. A Reg A deal will have an offering circular. Either way: the document is
where the real story is.
