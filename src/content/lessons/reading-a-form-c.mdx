Every Regulation Crowdfunding raise must file a **Form C** with the SEC. It's the
company's official disclosure, and it's public. Most investors skim the pitch
video and skip the Form C — which is backwards. The pitch is marketing; the Form
C is the closest thing to the truth on file.

## Where to find it

Form C filings are on the SEC's **EDGAR** database, and the funding portal links
to them on the deal page. If you can't find a Form C for a Reg CF deal, that's a
red flag in itself.

## The sections worth your time

- **The offering terms.** How much they're raising, the price, the security type
  (equity, SAFE, note), and the valuation. This is where you check whether the
  [cap table](/lessons/how-cap-tables-work/) math makes sense.
- **Use of proceeds.** What they'll spend the money on. "General working capital"
  for the whole raise is vaguer than you want.
- **Financial statements.** Revenue (if any), expenses, and how much cash they
  have. A company with months of runway raising a small amount is a different bet
  than a profitable one.
- **The risk factors.** Companies must disclose what could go wrong. Read these
  closely — the specific, unusual risks (not the boilerplate) tell you what the
  company is actually worried about.
- **Related-party transactions.** Money flowing to founders, family, or insiders.
  Worth understanding before you fund it.
- **The team.** Who's running it and what they've done before.

## How to read it skeptically

1. Cross-check the **valuation** against the financials. A pre-revenue company at
   a very high valuation is pricing in a lot of hope.
2. Compare **use of proceeds** to the pitch. Do the dollars go where the story
   says?
3. Read **risk factors** for anything specific and alarming, not just generic.
4. Note the **security type** — a SAFE with a high cap behaves very differently
   from priced equity.

> The Form C won't tell you a deal is good. It will tell you whether the deal is
> what the marketing claims — and that's most of the battle.
