There's no formula that turns a startup into a safe bet — most early-stage
companies fail, and that's the baseline reality. But you *can* tell a
better-structured, more honest deal from a worse one. Here's a practical way to
work through one.

## Start with the document, not the video

Pull the [Form C](/lessons/reading-a-form-c/) (Reg CF) or offering circular (Reg
A). The pitch is designed to excite you; the filing is designed to be accurate.
Read the filing first so the pitch can't anchor you.

## The valuation question

This is the one most retail investors skip and most regret.

- What's the **valuation**, and how does it compare to the company's actual
  revenue and stage? A pre-revenue company at a nine-figure valuation is pricing
  in enormous future success.
- Crowdfunding rounds are frequently priced **above** comparable professional
  (VC) rounds. Ask why this one deserves its number.
- Cross-check it against the [cap table](/lessons/how-cap-tables-work/): what do
  you actually own, fully diluted, for your money?

## The business itself

- **Revenue and growth** — real, or projected? Projections are not results.
- **Runway** — how long does this money last, and what happens when it runs out?
  Most companies will need to raise again (more [dilution](/lessons/dilution-explained/)).
- **The team** — have they built and exited something before, or is this a
  first-time founder (common in crowdfunding)?
- **Use of proceeds** — specific and sensible, or vague?

## The structure

- **Security type** — equity, SAFE, or note? Each behaves differently when the
  company raises again or exits.
- **Liquidity** — assume you cannot sell for many years, possibly never. Only
  invest money you can lock up.
- **Who else is in** — are professional investors participating on the same
  terms, or just the crowd?

## The promotion

- Who's marketing this deal, and are they **paid** to? Heavy promotion is about
  filling the raise, not about quality.
- If a newsletter or influencer is pushing it, check their actual record — that's
  what the [performance board](/gurus/) is for.

## A simple gut check

> If you can't explain, in one sentence, *what you own* and *how this could
> realistically return money*, you don't understand the deal well enough to fund
> it yet.

Sizing matters too: spread small amounts across many deals rather than betting
big on one, because the failure rate is high no matter how good your process is.
